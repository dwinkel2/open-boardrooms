# Positioning Boardroom — Course Context (optional, one time)

This is the one block that aims the whole board at your class. Fill in the brackets, save this file as `~/open-boardrooms/course-context.md` (or paste the block at the top of a run), and every run reads it. It calibrates every advisor without softening any of them. Skip it and the board runs at sensible defaults.

```
COURSE CONTEXT (calibrate the board to this class):
- Course and level: [e.g. sophomore Intro to Entrepreneurship / senior New Venture Capstone]
- Stage of our ventures: [idea only / building a prototype / have first customers]
- What counts as a real venture here: [e.g. must have a named customer segment and a
  real competitive alternative, not a hypothetical "someday everyone" market]
- Non-negotiables I teach (protect and enforce these):
    [e.g. position against a real alternative, name ONE best-fit customer, proof over
     adjectives, no "for everyone" positioning]
- Constraints my students live with:
    [e.g. no budget, no customers yet, one semester, team of 3]
```

The non-negotiables line does the most work. Name the two or three things you do not want a student talked out of, and the board enforces them instead of debating them.

---

## Worked example (a filled-in default you can adapt)

Here is the same block, filled in for a real-feeling course. Copy it, change the details to match your class, and you are done.

```
COURSE CONTEXT (calibrate the board to this class):
- Course and level: EN 320 New Venture Creation, a junior and senior experiential
  entrepreneurship course. Mixed majors, not just business students.
- Stage of our ventures: Building. Most have a rough prototype or a landing page and are
  in customer discovery. A few have their first paying customers.
- What counts as a real venture here: A named customer segment the student has actually
  talked to, and a real competitive alternative those people use today. "Someday everyone
  will want this" does not count. If they have not interviewed at least five people in the
  segment, treat the customer as a guess.
- Non-negotiables I teach (protect and enforce these):
    - Position against a real alternative, including "a spreadsheet" or "doing nothing,"
      never in a vacuum.
    - Name ONE best-fit customer. No "for everyone," no three segments at once.
    - Proof over adjectives. "Powerful" and "AI-powered" are not attributes. Force the
      concrete capability and the evidence for it.
    - The category is a deliberate choice. Make them defend the shelf they picked.
- Constraints my students live with: One 15-week semester, teams of 2 to 4, no budget, no
  engineering help beyond no-code tools, and most have never sold anything before.
```
