# Unit Economics Boardroom — Course Context (optional, one time)

This is the one block that aims the whole board at your class. Fill in the brackets, save this file as `~/open-boardrooms/course-context.md` (or paste the block at the top of a run), and every run reads it. It calibrates every advisor without softening any of them. Skip it and the board runs at sensible defaults.

```
COURSE CONTEXT (calibrate the board to this class):
- Course and level: [e.g. junior New Venture Development]
- Stage of our ventures: [idea with estimates / building / real revenue]
- What counts as a real number here: [e.g. a defended estimate with a source,
  not a number you made up]
- Non-negotiables I teach (enforce these):
    [e.g. use contribution margin, not revenue, in lifetime value;
     use the paid cost of a customer, not the blended one;
     count shipping, fees, and returns in cost]
- Constraints my students live with: [e.g. pre-revenue, no ad budget yet,
  one semester, team of 3]
```

The non-negotiables line does the most work. Whatever number your students always fudge, name it here, and the board enforces the honest version instead of debating it.

---

## Worked example (a filled-in default you can adapt)

Here is the same block, filled in for a real-feeling course. Copy it, change the details to match your class, and you are done.

```
COURSE CONTEXT (calibrate the board to this class):
- Course and level: EN 320 New Venture Creation, a junior and senior experiential
  entrepreneurship course. Mixed majors, not just business students.
- Stage of our ventures: Idea with estimates, moving toward first sales. Most have a price
  and rough costs. Few have real revenue data yet.
- What counts as a real number here: A defended estimate with a source. A supplier quote, a
  real ad-platform cost per click, a competitor's public price. A number made up in the
  spreadsheet does not count and should be marked as a guess.
- Non-negotiables I teach (enforce these):
    - Use contribution margin, not revenue, in lifetime value.
    - Use the paid cost of acquiring a customer, not the blended number that hides it.
    - Count everything in unit cost: shipping, payment fees, returns, and delivery labor.
    - No "we'll make it up on volume." If one customer loses money, scale loses more.
- Constraints my students live with: One 15-week semester, teams of 2 to 4, no ad budget,
  pre-revenue or barely post-revenue, and most have never built a financial model before.
```
