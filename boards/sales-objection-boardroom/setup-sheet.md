```
# 1) COURSE CONTEXT (your instructor may give you this; if not, delete this section)
COURSE CONTEXT (calibrate the board to this class):

- Non-negotiables: honest selling only; ground every rebuttal in something true;
  never push a buyer past a real problem; if an objection has no honest answer,
  say the offer must change.

# 2) YOUR PITCH (fill in every bracket; be specific)
MY PITCH:
- What I sell, and the price: [...]
- Who I sell to: [the buyer, their role, who actually signs off]
- My sales motion and where objections hit: [demo / call / proposal / close]
- My current pitch or how I answer objections now: [paste it, or "nothing written yet"]
- The real proof I have: [results, guarantee, references, ROI, differentiator]
- My walk-away: [the concession I will NOT make, e.g. a price floor]

# 3) BOARD PROMPT (do not edit anything from here down; it runs the board)
You are running a Sales Objection Boardroom on the pitch above. This is a legitimate
sales-practice exercise, and its purpose is HONEST objection handling. The goal is to help
the seller understand real buyer concerns and answer them truthfully, so the right buyer
buys with clear eyes and the wrong buyer walks away clean. A rebuttal is a true, useful
answer to a real concern, never a trick to push someone past a valid problem. If an
objection has no honest answer because the offer itself is thin (weak proof, wrong price,
poor fit), SAY SO: "fix the offer" is a valid result, and a slick line that hides a real
weakness is not.

FIRST, build a short baseline: the claims this pitch makes (each is a surface an objection
attacks), the real offer facts a rebuttal can honestly stand on, and the seller's walk-away.

BUILD THE ROOM. Generate 4-5 PROSPECTS from the seller's real buyer, each a specific person
with a name, a budget reality, a current alternative, and the one thing that would make them
walk. Between them they must cover: price/budget, status-quo/incumbent, trust/proof,
timing/stall, authority/process, and fit/risk. At least one controls the money; at least one
is happy with their incumbent.

AND SEAT FIVE COACHES, each in their own voice, who disagree on how to handle a no:
- CHRIS VOSS (tactical empathy): label the emotion, accusation audit, calibrated questions,
  aim for "that's right," never argue.
- JEB BLOUNT (framework): separate the reflexive brush-off from the real objection, stay
  composed, ask before answering.
- MATTHEW DIXON (Challenger): do not answer inside the buyer's frame; reframe why their
  status quo is costing them; teach, take control.
- THE SANDLER REP (pull-back): never chase; negative reverse selling; "maybe this isn't a
  fit for you"; disqualify fast.
- GRANT CARDONE (pressure): the sale is made in the objection; ask again; sell value harder.
  But even here, never push a buyer past a real, unanswered problem.

RUN IT IN ROUNDS:

ROUND 1, THE BARRAGE. Each prospect raises every objection they'd really raise, in their
voice. For each: what they SAY out loud, what they actually MEAN underneath, and how the
current pitch handles it: HANDLED / WEAK / DODGED. Each names the objection that would make
them walk. Then compile the master objection list, tagged by category, and show the DODGED
list (the objections the pitch never answers).

ROUND 2, REBUTTALS, THEN THE BUYERS JUDGE. For the top objections, each coach writes their
rebuttal, word for word, their own way, grounded in a real offer fact, respecting the
walk-away. Do not blend the coaches. THEN each prospect judges the rebuttals aimed at them:
for each, LANDS / SHAKY / BACKFIRES, and why, in the buyer's voice. A rebuttal only wins if
the buyer who raised the objection says it would move them.

ROUND 3, WINNERS AND DODGES. For each objection, give the rebuttal the buyers rewarded,
refined, word for word, with a delivery note and a "do not say" note. For any objection where
every rebuttal backfired, state plainly that this is an OFFER problem, not a pitch problem,
and name the upstream change (proof, price, fit) that would dissolve it.

THEN HAND ME THREE THINGS:
1. THE OBJECTION PLAYBOOK: every objection ranked by deal impact: what they say, what they
   mean, the winning rebuttal (word for word), what it stands on, delivery, what not to say.
   Plus the DODGED objections ranked, and the objections no rebuttal can fix (offer problems).
2. THE CHANGELOG: the lines to add or cut from my pitch so it preempts the objections it
   now dodges.
3. THE BOARD REPORT: the buyers' scoreboard (which coach's rebuttal landed, per the
   prospects), the toughest buyer, and any rebuttal a coach swears by that the buyers rejected.

Start with the baseline, then Round 1. After each round, pause and wait for me to type
"continue."
```

Read the output in this order: the DODGED objections first (the ones you didn't know you were losing on), then the objections no rebuttal can fix (the offer problems), then the winning rebuttals, then the changelog.

## Spar (do this after the playbook)

The playbook is the study guide. Sparring is the practice. Paste this to go live.

```
Now run a LIVE SPARRING session. You play one of my prospects (pick my toughest, or ask me
which). Stay fully in that buyer's voice. Throw ONE objection, then STOP and wait for me to
type my response. Do not answer for me. React the way that buyer really would: if my answer
lands, soften and move to your next concern; if it's weak, push back harder; if it
backfires, start to leave. After each exchange, give me one honest coaching note (what
worked, or the fix). Continue for several exchanges until you buy, book a next step, or walk.
Then score how I did and give me one adjustment. Difficulty: [warm / realistic / brutal].
Hold me to honest answers. If I try to spin past a real problem, call it.
```
